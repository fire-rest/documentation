title: "Out of stock"
description: "Turn off a product that ran out mid-service and let it come back on its own, without touching the menu or relying on someone remembering."
---

It is two in the afternoon and the kitchen has run out of fries. The product is still selling, orders keep coming in, and somebody is going to have to call the customer and tell them there are none. Until now the alternative was editing the menu: slow, risky, and with a guarantee that at six, when the delivery arrives, nobody will remember to undo it.

This screen exists for that. Turn the product off for a while, with a reason, and it comes back on its own. Go to **Restaurant OS → Operations → Out of stock**.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/out-of-stock/01-listado.png" alt="List of a store's products with their selling status" />
</Frame>

## The minimum you need to know

<Note>
**1. You are not editing the menu.** The published menu is untouched. This is a separate, temporary layer that switches itself off. Nobody has to remember to undo it.

**2. Every turn-off has an expiry, and you choose it.** From 30 minutes to a date days away, with no maximum. When the time comes, the product sells again by itself.

**3. The reason is mandatory and cannot be edited afterwards.** It is the only thing left to explain why the product was turned off. Write it for whoever reads it tomorrow.

**4. The turn-off travels to the channels where the product sells.** Turning off, restoring and expiring all reach the kiosk, the till and the aggregators. The screen tells you if one of them did not get the word.

**5. A product that is an option of a combo's required question is protected.** The screen does not let you leave a combo on sale with no option to pick. It blocks in red, asks for an action in amber and only informs in gray.
</Note>

---

## The simple path

Something ran out and you want it out of sale now:

1. Pick the store in the selector at the top right.
2. Find the product and open the three-dot menu on its row.
3. **Turn off temporarily**.
4. Leave the duration at **1 hour**, write the reason and confirm with **Turn off**.

Done. The product goes to **Turned off**, the **Until** column shows what time it comes back, and it comes back on its own.

<Tip>
If this is your case — it ran out, I turn it off, it comes back when the delivery arrives — you are finished. The rest of this manual is for when the turn-off has to last until closing, affect only one channel, or the product is also part of a combo.
</Tip>

## How long the turn-off lasts

The first thing you decide when turning a product off is how long.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/out-of-stock/02-apagar.png" alt="Turn-off dialog with the duration, scope and reason options" />
</Frame>

| Option | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **30 min** · **1 hour** · **2 hours** | Counts from now |
| **Until closing** | Until the store closes its service day |
| **Date and time** | A date and a time that you pick, at least 5 minutes from now |

The same line always appears below: **"Comes back today at…"** (or **"Comes back on…"** when it is another day), with the time and how long is left. It is the confirmation of what you just picked.

### There is no ceiling

A turn-off lasts until any future moment you choose. **Until closing** is only a shortcut for the store's closing time; with **Date and time** you can pick tomorrow, next week or the 30th, and there is no maximum.

<Tip>
An ice cream that is out of stock until the 30th stays off until the 30th: pick **Date and time** once and it comes back by itself that day. Write the reason with that in mind, because whoever reads the **Audit** next week needs to know why it was off for days. If the product is gone for good, the right tool is still the menu, not a long turn-off.
</Tip>

### Changing the duration without starting over

If the turn-off falls short, the three-dot menu has **Change expiry**. It moves the time and nothing else.

If the new time would leave a combo coming back on sale without options — the combo itself brought forward, or one of its options pushed past the time the combo comes back — the dialog warns you:

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/out-of-stock/14-reprogramar-aviso.png" alt="Change expiry dialog with the amber notice: 1 Batata Grande would go back on sale without options" />
</Frame>

*"1 Batata Grande would go back on sale without options"*, with the note *"This is only a warning: you can still save the new time"*. **Saving is not blocked**: you may be rearranging several turn-offs one at a time. Here there is no **Extend** button, because you are already choosing the time.

<Warning>
**The reason cannot be changed.** **Change expiry** only moves the time. The reason is the evidence of why the product was turned off, and rewriting it while the turn-off is still live would leave a record that does not match what happened. If the reason genuinely changed, restore the product and turn it off again.
</Warning>

## The times are on your clock

**Which clock are the times on screen written in?** Yours. All of them — the dialog, the **Until** column, the detail and the **Audit** — are shown in your device's time zone, the same as everywhere else in the panel. You will never see two different times for the same turn-off. When the turn-off ends on a day other than today, the **Until** column also shows the date.

The store's clock only comes in through **Until closing**, which uses that store's closing time and shows it to you on your own clock. A São Paulo store that closes at 22:00, operated from Ecuador — two hours behind — shows *"Comes back today at 08:00 PM"*. These are not two schedules: it is the same instant, written on the clock in front of you.

## Where it gets turned off: the channels

A product can have run out in one channel and not in another. The **Where?** block decides the scope.

- **Every channel in this store** — the product stops selling across the whole store.
- **Only some channels** — you tick the channel and Fulfillment Type pairs affected.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/out-of-stock/03-canales.png" alt="Picking specific channels inside the turn-off dialog" />
</Frame>

Each checkbox is a **channel · Fulfillment Type** pair: *iFood · Delivery*, *Kiosk · Takeaway*, *Kiosk · Dine-In*, *Point of Sale · Takeaway*, *Point of Sale · Dine-In*. The channels listed are the ones that store has configured.

The pairs come grouped, and the channels in one group are notified together: pick the whole group and it turns off right away; pick only part of it and it is applied with a menu sync, which takes about a minute.

In the table, the **Scope** column shows **All channels** or the specific pair, so you can see at a glance how far each turn-off reaches.

<Warning>
**Turning off too much costs sales.** If the product ran out for takeaway but there is still some for dine-in, picking **Every channel in this store** stops you selling where you did have product. The broad scope is the convenient one to tick and the one that leaves the most money on the table.
</Warning>

### The scope decides who gets told

The turn-off is only sent to the channels the scope touches. A turn-off on **Kiosk · Takeaway** says nothing to the till or to the aggregators. A turn-off on **Every channel in this store** talks to every channel that store has configured.

Each kind of channel receives the word its own way, but that is the system's business: what you pick is the scope.

## When the channel does not know the menu yet

A product can be in the menu assigned to the store and never have reached the channel, because that menu has not been published yet. Over there the product does not exist, so there is nothing to turn off — and the attempt is rejected before anything is saved:

> Ninguno de los canales de este alcance tiene el menú de la tienda sincronizado, así que allá el producto no existe y el apagado no tendría efecto. Sincronizá el menú y volvé a intentarlo.

Products are listed from the assigned menu on purpose, so they show up even before publishing. Publish the store's menu and the turn-off becomes available.

<Note>
A channel that is **unconfigured or disabled** blocks nothing: it is not the destination of any notification, and that is about the account's configuration, not about the product.
</Note>

### The other two rejections

| Message | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| *"Ese producto ya tiene un apagado vigente que se superpone con este alcance"* | Restore the turn-off that already exists, then turn the product off again with the scope you want |
| *"Ese producto no se vende en alguno de los canales seleccionados"* | Untick that pair: the store does not have it assigned |

## When the product is also a side

A product does not always sell on its own: it can be an option of another product's **required question**, the one the customer has to answer before ordering. That other product is a combo, or a product with modifiers. The fries in **1 Batata Grande** are the example: its question *"Escolha sua batata"* only offers **Batata Média**. Turn Batata Média off and that combo has nothing left to offer, so it can no longer be ordered.

The screen protects the combo from that, before you confirm anything:

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/out-of-stock/04-impacto-bloqueo.png" alt="Turn-off dialog with a red block: 1 Batata Grande could no longer be ordered" />
</Frame>

The impact block uses three colors, and each means something different:

| Color | What it means | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Red** | Blocks: you cannot confirm until you resolve it | A combo would be left with no option to pick |
| **Amber** | Asks for an action: decide before confirming | A combo comes back before the option it needs |
| **Gray** | Only informs | The combo's "from" price goes up |

### What blocks the turn-off

Turning off is **blocked** when it would leave a combo that is on sale with a required question that has **no available option at all**, in some channel and Fulfillment Type of the scope you picked. The dialog shows it in red before you confirm, and the server rejects it anyway if someone insists.

One available option is enough, even if the question asks for 2 or 4 units, because the same option can be repeated. That is why the block says *"requires 1 and 0 would be left"*: what counts is that it reaches zero.

To continue you have two ways out: turn off the combo too (next section), or remove from the scope the channels where it would be left empty.

### Know beforehand what blocks

You do not have to open the dialog to find out. In the table, next to the product name:

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/out-of-stock/08-insignias.png" alt="Table with the Inside N products chip and the Blocks N products and Changes price of N badges" />
</Frame>

- **Blocks N products** (red): turning it off in the whole store would leave N products that cannot be ordered.
- **Changes price of N** (gray): turning it off raises the reference price of N combos. It does not block.
- **Inside N products**: the product is an option of N products. Click it for the detail.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/out-of-stock/09-popover.png" alt="Detail of the products that offer it, per channel and Fulfillment Type" />
</Frame>

The detail lists each combo, its question and, for every channel and Fulfillment Type, what would happen: **would be left empty** in red when it would block, and in gray **N of M available** or **minimum X → Y** (the combo's lowest price, before and after) when it only informs.

### Turn off the option and the combo together

If the combo also has to leave the sale, the red block offers **Add 1 Batata Grande to this selection**. Turning off the option and its combo together **is allowed**.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/out-of-stock/10-impacto-agregado.png" alt="The block turns green and the button reads Turn off 2 products" />
</Frame>

The block turns green, *"1 Batata Grande is turned off together with this selection"*, and the button reads **Turn off 2 products**. Both get the same expiry, scope and reason, but each stays as its own row: restoring one does not move the other. **Remove from selection** takes the combo back out.

### When the "from" price goes up

If the option you turn off was the cheapest in a combo's question, the combo's reference price goes up: the minimum a customer can pay is now higher.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/out-of-stock/11-impacto-precio.png" alt="Gray notice: the from price is not updated, with from and real minimum per combo" />
</Frame>

The gray notice shows, per combo, *"from 72.90 · real minimum 84.80"*. It is **for information only**: nothing is sent to the channel, which keeps showing the published "from" price, and it does not block. The first figure is today's minimum, counting the options that are already off, so the line shows what **this** turn-off moves.

### The combo that comes back before the option

If the combo is already turned off but comes back **earlier** than the option you are turning off, the combo would return to sale with its question empty. The dialog warns in amber:

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/out-of-stock/12-impacto-vuelve.png" alt="Amber block: 1 Batata Grande comes back before this option, with the Extend button" />
</Frame>

The button **Extend 1 Batata Grande until 05:28 PM** fixes it. The suggested time is the first moment the question has an option again.

<Warning>
**Extend moves the whole turn-off of the combo, in all its channels**, not only where the option runs out. The dialog says it: *"It is extended in every channel of that turn-off."*
</Warning>

### What this protection does not do

<Note>
**Automatic expiry is never blocked.** If the combo's time comes, it goes back on sale even if its question is still empty. That is why the amber notices exist: this screen cannot stop an expiry, only warn you before.

**Channels that never synced the menu do not count.** The check uses the menu published in each channel and Fulfillment Type. Where the menu is assigned but was never synced, there is no block and no warning.
</Note>

## Selling again

There are two paths, and the normal one is to do nothing.

**It expires on its own.** When the time comes, the product sells again with nobody stepping in. It is the default behaviour and the reason the duration is mandatory.

**Restore now.** If the problem was solved earlier, the three-dot menu has **Restore now** and the product comes back immediately.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/out-of-stock/05-apagados.png" alt="Table of turned-off products, the time they come back and the bulk action bar" />
</Frame>

The header holds the store's counters: how many products are **selling**, **off**, **not selling** (their menu is not published yet) and **expiring** within the hour. Two more appear only when there is something to deal with: **not re-enabled** and **not notified**. The first four are the picture of the service; the last two are things somebody has to resolve.

The filters above the table are four: **All**, **Turned off**, **Expiring soon** and **Not turned back on**.

The **Until** column shows **the time**, not a countdown. A countdown forces you to do the arithmetic in your head and goes stale the moment you look away.

### Turning several off at once

Ticking the checkboxes on several rows brings up **Turn off temporarily (N)** at the top. The dialog is the same, and what you pick applies to everything selected: same duration, same scope, same reason.

Only products that are selling go in. The ones already turned off are ignored, so there is no risk of overwriting an existing turn-off by over-selecting.

### Restoring a combo that still has no options

Restoring by hand a combo whose required question still has no available option is **blocked**. The dialog **"1 Batata Grande cannot be restored"** lists the options that are turned off, each with the time it comes back:

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/out-of-stock/13-restaurar-bloqueado.png" alt="Dialog: 1 Batata Grande cannot be restored, with the Restore Batata Média and 1 Batata Grande button" />
</Frame>

The shortcut **Restore Batata Média and 1 Batata Grande** restores the option first, the one that expires earliest, and then the combo. You can also restore the option by hand and come back to the combo.

<Note>
The block only applies to restoring by hand. Automatic expiry is never blocked, and neither is **Turn back on now** on a turn-off whose time already passed.
</Note>

## When the product was never turned back on

The automatic turn-back-on rides on a single scheduled task. If that task fails, the product shows as selling again here while **the channel still has it turned off**. That is the dangerous case: the screen is wrong in the direction that costs sales.

That is why it flags itself:

- A counter in the header: **N not re-enabled**.
- A **Not turned back on** filter, to isolate them.
- On the row, the status **Still off in the channel**, with the last attempt's error on hover.
- The **Turn back on now** action, which is the real fix: it closes the turn-off **and** sends the channel the switch-on the task failed to send.

<Warning>
**A product that says "Selling" is not always selling.** If you see that counter, deal with it before the shift change: every row there is a product the channel is not offering and nobody decided to leave off.
</Warning>

## When a channel did not get the word

Saving the turn-off and telling the channel are two different things, and they are reported separately: first the notice that the product was turned off, then a **second notice** about the channels.

| Notice | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Availability sent to the channels** | It reached all of them |
| **Sent, but some channels are not configured yet** | It reached some; others are missing configuration |
| **The channels are not fully configured: nothing was sent** | Configuration is missing on all of them |
| **Part of the change could not be sent to the channels** | Some channel rejected the notification |
| **The channels could not be notified** | None of them received it |

In every case **the turn-off was saved**: a channel being down never reads as a failed turn-off. And the verdict does not leave with the notice — live turn-offs that did not reach some channel are counted in the header as **N not notified** and carry the **A channel was not notified** mark on their row.

<Note>
The notice says **sent**, never "applied". A channel can accept the notification and take a while to reflect it.
</Note>

## Publishing the menu does not undo the turn-off

Publishing the store's menu respects the live turn-offs: the turned-off product goes out in that publish as **not available**, in every channel and Fulfillment Type the turn-off covers. This applies to X-Mart channels and to aggregators alike. A whole-store turn-off also reaches a channel that was assigned after the turn-off was made.

That makes publishing safe in the middle of service:

- **What is turned off stays turned off.** No publish closes a turn-off: it lasts until it expires or someone restores it.
- **It comes back at its time.** Its scheduled turn-back-on stays in place.

<Note>
If the live turn-offs cannot be read when publishing, **the publish fails** instead of going out without them: publishing without that reading would put everything that is turned off back on sale.
</Note>

## The Audit tab

**Audit** keeps every turn-off in the store and how each one ended.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/out-of-stock/07-auditoria.png" alt="History of turn-offs with product, scope, times, outcome, user and reason" />
</Frame>

| Outcome | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| **Active** | Still turned off right now |
| **Restored** | Somebody put it back on sale by hand |
| **Expired** | The duration ran out and it came back on its own |
| **Replaced by a sync** | Only appears on turn-offs from before this change: today no publish closes a turn-off |

Every row carries the user and the reason. It is the answer to *"why didn't we sell fries yesterday between two and six?"*, and it is why the reason is mandatory.

The detail of a live turn-off opens from **View detail**:

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/out-of-stock/06-detalle.png" alt="Turn-off detail: scope, start, expiry, user, origin and reason" />
</Frame>

The **Via** field says where the product was turned off from. **Fire** is the panel; a turn-off done from the point of sale shows its own origin, and is restored from here just the same.

## Recipes: how the real cases get solved

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The fries ran out halfway through lunch">
    1. Pick the store in the selector at the top.
    2. Type "fries" in the search box.
    3. Open the three-dot menu on the row and pick **Turn off temporarily**.
    4. Duration **1 hour** — what you reckon the delivery takes — and scope **Every channel in this store**.
    5. Reason: *"Ran out of stock in the kitchen"*.
    6. **Turn off**.

    The second notice tells you whether the channels got the word. If the delivery arrives earlier, **Restore now**. If it takes longer, **Change expiry**.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Turning twelve products off at once when the hot kitchen closes">
    1. Filter or search so the table holds that station's products.
    2. Tick all of their checkboxes.
    3. **Turn off temporarily (12)** appears at the top.
    4. Duration **Until closing**: the hot kitchen is not opening again today.
    5. Reason: *"Hot kitchen closed for the day"*.
    6. **Turn off**.

    All twelve end up turned off with the same duration and the same reason, and tomorrow they wake up selling.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="It ran out at the kiosk, but there is still some at the till">
    1. Open **Turn off temporarily** on the product.
    2. Under **Where?**, pick **Only some channels**.
    3. Tick only the kiosk pairs: *Kiosk · Takeaway* and *Kiosk · Dine-In*.
    4. Reason, then **Turn off**.

    In the table, the **Scope** column stops saying "All channels" and shows the specific pair. The notification travels only to the kiosk: the product keeps selling at the till.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="No delivery today: let it come back tomorrow on its own">
    1. Open **Turn off temporarily**.
    2. Duration **Until closing**.
    3. Reason: *"No supplier delivery, restocking tomorrow"*.
    4. **Turn off**.

    The turn-off ends at the store's closing. When you open tomorrow the product is selling again, so if the delivery did not arrive it has to be turned off once more — nobody is going to warn you. If you already know it will not arrive until Thursday, pick **Date and time** instead and set Thursday.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The fries ran out and they are inside combos">
    1. Find the product (for example **Batata Média**) and open its three-dot menu.
    2. **Turn off temporarily**, with the duration, scope and reason you need.
    3. Read the **Impact on products that offer it** block before confirming:
       - **Red** — *"1 Batata Grande could no longer be ordered"*. If that combo should not be sold either, use **Add 1 Batata Grande to this selection**: the block turns green and the button reads **Turn off 2 products**. If it has to keep selling, remove from the scope the channels the block lists.
       - **Amber** — a combo comes back before the option. Use **Extend** to move its turn-off to the suggested time, or confirm knowing it will go back on sale with its question empty.
       - **Gray** — a combo's "from" price goes up. It informs; nothing is sent to the channel.
    4. **Turn off** (or **Turn off 2 products**).

    To find out beforehand, look at the badges next to the product name: **Blocks N products** and **Changes price of N**.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="It was solved sooner than expected">
    1. Filter by **Turned off** to see only what is out of sale.
    2. Open the product's three-dot menu.
    3. **Restore now**.

    It sells again immediately, and the switch-on goes out to the channels in scope. In **Audit** it stays as **Restored**, with the real time it came back.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The supplier is late and the turn-off falls short">
    1. Open the three-dot menu on the turned-off product.
    2. **Change expiry**.
    3. Pick the new time and save.

    Only the time moves: the original reason stays as it was. If what changed is the reason — it is no longer "out of stock" but "broken fryer" — restore the product and turn it off again with the right reason.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The header says there are products that were not turned back on">
    1. Filter by **Not turned back on**.
    2. Go through each row: the **Still off in the channel** status shows the last attempt's error on hover.
    3. In the three-dot menu, **Turn back on now**.

    That closes the turn-off and sends the channel the switch-on that never went out. Do it before the shift change: until then, those products are not being offered.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="It will not let me turn a product off: it says the menu is not synced">
    1. Note which store it happened in, and with what scope.
    2. Go to that store's menu publishing and publish it.
    3. Come back to **Out of stock** and try the turn-off again.

    The product shows in the list because it is in the menu assigned to the store, but while that menu is unpublished the channel does not know it and there is nothing to turn off over there.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## One service, hour by hour

A store that closes at 22:00. This is what the operator sees at each point:

| Time | What happens | Status in store | **Until** column |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 12:40 | The fries run out. **1 hour** turn-off, every channel | **Turned off** | 13:40 |
| 13:10 | The delivery is late. **Change expiry** to 15:00 | **Turned off** | 15:00 |
| 14:20 | The delivery arrives. **Restore now** | **Selling** | — |
| 19:00 | They run out again, no more deliveries. **Until closing** | **Turned off** | 22:00 |
| 22:00 | The service day closes. The turn-off expires on its own | **Selling** | — |

Both entries stay in **Audit**: the first as **Restored** at 14:20, the second as **Expired** at 22:00. Nobody had to remember anything.

And if at 19:00 they had known there would be none tomorrow either, they could have picked **Date and time**, tomorrow at noon, and the product would have come back by itself then.

## Mistakes that cost money

<Warning>
**Turning off every channel when it was missing in only one.** It is the option ticked by default and the fastest one to confirm. If the product only ran out at the kiosk, you also stop selling it at the till and in the dining room, where you did have it.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**A generic reason.** *"Test"*, *"out of stock"*, *"xx"*. The reason is the only thing left in the history and it **cannot be edited**. When somebody asks a month from now why that product was out of sale for four hours, that text is the whole answer there is going to be.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**Ignoring the counters on the right.** **Not re-enabled** and **not notified** are not informational: they are products whose state in the channel does not match what the screen says. Each one is either a sale that is not happening, or a sale of something you do not have.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**Confirming over the amber notice.** When a combo comes back before the option it needs, the screen warns but does not block, and **automatic expiry is never blocked**: at the combo's time it goes back on sale even if its required question still has no options. Extend the combo before confirming, or accept that gap knowingly. And remember that **Extend** moves the combo's whole turn-off, in all its channels, not only where the option runs out.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**Reading out the time you see over the phone, from a different country than the store.** What the screen shows is written on your clock, not the store's. If you tell the manager "it comes back at eight" and they are two hours ahead, each of you is talking about a different moment.
</Warning>

## Glossary

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Temporary turn-off** | Taking a product out of sale in one store, for a while and with an expiry. It does not touch the menu. |
| **Duration** | What time the turn-off lasts until. Picked when turning off, and movable afterwards. |
| **Service day** | The store's working day, with its closing time. It is the time that **Until closing** uses. |
| **Scope** | Where the turn-off applies: the whole store, or only certain channels. It also decides which channels get told. |
| **Channel** | Where the sale comes in: kiosk, point of sale, aggregators, and whatever the store has configured. |
| **Fulfillment Type** | How the order is handed over: takeaway, dine-in, delivery. It combines with the channel. |
| **Reason** | Why it was turned off. Mandatory, minimum five characters, cannot be edited afterwards. |
| **Modifier group** | The set of options a product offers, such as the fries of a combo. It can be required or optional. |
| **Required question** | A modifier group with a minimum of 1 or more: the customer has to choose before ordering. If it has no available option, the product cannot be ordered. |
| **Audit** | The history of every turn-off in the store and how each one ended. |
| **Restore** | Putting the product back on sale before the duration runs out. |
| **Not turned back on** | A turn-off whose duration expired but whose switch-on never reached the channel. Has to be resolved by hand. |
| **Not notified** | A live turn-off that did not reach one of the channels in its scope. |
| **Menu sync** | Publishing the store's menu to its channels, respecting the live turn-offs. It no longer closes any of them. |
| **Replaced by a sync** | A historical outcome that is no longer produced. It only shows in the **Audit** on turn-offs from before the change. |
| **Via** | Where the product was turned off from: the panel (**Fire**) or the point of sale. |

## Frequently asked questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="How long can a turn-off last?">
    As long as you decide: any future moment, with no maximum. **Until closing** is a shortcut for the store's closing time, and **Date and time** takes any date and time at least 5 minutes from now.

    If the product is going to be missing for good, the right move is still to take it out of the menu.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Does the turn-off reach the sales channels?">
    Yes. Turning off, restoring and expiring all travel to the channels in scope, each with its own mechanism: X-Mart channels get the product listing, and aggregators get an availability event per channel.

    What stays here is the record: this screen is Fire's operational account of what stopped being sold, when and why. It never becomes part of the menu — the menu still says the product exists, and the turn-off says it is not selling right now.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How do I know the channel got the word?">
    From the second notice that appears when you save, and from the table header. If some channel was left uninformed, the turn-off adds to the **not notified** counter and its row carries the **A channel was not notified** mark. The turn-off was still saved: what is missing is the notification, not the turn-off.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What time zone are the times I see in?">
    Your device's, on every screen: the dialog, the **Until** column, the detail and the **Audit** all use the same clock, so you will never see two different times for the same turn-off.

    What does not depend on your clock is **Until closing**: it uses the store's closing, and if you are in another country you see it translated into your time.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What happens if nobody restores the product?">
    It sells again on its own once the duration runs out. That is the point: the turn-off does not need anyone to remember to undo it. In **Audit** it stays as **Expired**.

    If the switch-on never made it to the channel, the product shows up in the **Not turned back on** filter and is resolved with **Turn back on now**.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="A product says Selling but the channel is not offering it">
    That is a turn-back-on that never went out. Filter by **Not turned back on**: it shows there with the **Still off in the channel** status and the last attempt's error. **Turn back on now** resolves it and sends the channel the switch-on that was missing.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What happens to the turn-offs if I publish the store's menu?">
    Nothing happens to them. Publishing respects the live turn-offs: the product goes out as not available in every channel and Fulfillment Type the turn-off covers, and no publish closes it. It stays off until it expires or someone restores it, and its scheduled turn-back-on stays in place.

    If the live turn-offs cannot be read when publishing, the publish fails instead of going out without them.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="It rejects my turn-off because the menu is not synced">
    The product is in the menu assigned to the store, but that menu has not been published to the channels in that scope yet. Over there the product does not exist, so there is nothing to turn off. Publish the store's menu and try again.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I search for a product and it does not show in the list">
    The list shows what that store actually sells: the products in the menu it has published or assigned. If the product is not in that menu, it cannot be turned off because it is not selling there.

    Check which menu the product is in, and which menu the store has assigned.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The store says it has no menu assigned">
    That shows up when the store has no menu yet, and then there is nothing to turn off. The **Go to menu assignments** button takes you straight to fixing it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="It will not let me turn a product off: it says a combo would be left without options">
    The product is an option of a required question, and turning it off would leave a combo that is on sale with no available option in some channel and Fulfillment Type of your scope. You have two ways out: **Add the combo to this selection** and turn both off, or remove from the scope the channels the block lists. One available option is enough for it not to block, even if the question asks for several units.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="It will not let me restore a combo">
    Its required question still has no available option, so putting it back on sale would leave it unorderable. The dialog **"1 Batata Grande cannot be restored"** (with the name of your combo) lists the options that are turned off. Use **Restore Batata Média and 1 Batata Grande**, or restore the option first.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The combo's from price did not change after turning off its cheapest option">
    The gray notice says so: it is **for information only**. The channel keeps showing the published "from" price, and nothing is sent to it. The real minimum a customer can pay is the one the notice shows.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I restore a turn-off that was made from the point of sale?">
    Yes. A live turn-off is operational state, not the property of whoever created it. The **Via** field in the detail says where it was made from, but it does not limit who can put the product back on sale.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
