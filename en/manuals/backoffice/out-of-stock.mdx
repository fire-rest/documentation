description: "Turn off a product that ran out mid-service and let it come back on its own, without touching the menu or relying on anyone to remember."
---

It is two in the afternoon and the kitchen has run out of fries. The product is still on sale, orders keep coming in, and somebody is going to have to call a customer and tell them it is not available. Until now the only way out was to edit the menu: slow, risky, and with a guarantee that at six, when the delivery arrives, nobody will remember to undo it.

This screen exists for that. Turn the product off for a while, with a reason, and it comes back by itself. Go to **Restaurant OS → Operation → Out of stock**.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/out-of-stock/01-listado.png" alt="A store's product list showing what is selling and what is turned off" />
</Frame>

## The short version

<Note>
**1. You are not editing the menu.** The published menu stays untouched. This is a separate, temporary layer that switches itself off. Nobody has to remember to undo it.

**2. Every turn-off has an expiry, and it never outlives the store's closing time.** If the store closes at 10:00 PM, no turn-off survives that hour. The next day the product wakes up selling.

**3. The reason is mandatory and cannot be edited afterwards.** It is the only record of why the product was turned off. Write it for whoever reads it tomorrow.
</Note>

---

## The simple path

Something ran out and you want it off the menu now:

1. Pick the store in the selector at the top right.
2. Search for the product and open the three-dot menu on its row.
3. **Turn off temporarily**.
4. Leave the expiry at **1 hour**, write the reason and confirm with **Turn off**.

Done. The product shows as **Turned off**, the **Until** column shows what time it comes back, and it comes back on its own.

<Tip>
If this is your case — it ran out, I turn it off, it returns when the delivery arrives — you are finished. The rest of this manual is for when the turn-off has to last until closing, or affect only one channel.
</Tip>

## How long the turn-off lasts

The first thing you decide when turning a product off is the expiry.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/out-of-stock/02-apagar.png" alt="Turn-off dialog with the expiry options, scope and reason" />
</Frame>

| Option | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **30 min** · **1 hour** · **2 hours** | Counts from now |
| **Until closing** | Until the store closes for the day |
| **Specific time** | A time today that you type in |

Below it, one line is always there: **"Comes back automatically at…"**, with the time and how long is left. It confirms what you just chose.

### The ceiling is the store's closing time

No turn-off can outlive the end of the business day. The dialog states it outright: *"It can never outlive today's service — the store closes at…"*, using that store's closing time.

That is why options that would run past closing **appear disabled**. Twenty minutes before closing, **1 hour** and **2 hours** cannot be picked: you are left with **30 min**, **Until closing**, and a **Specific time** before the close.

<Info>
**Why you cannot turn something off until tomorrow.** A product being out of stock is a fact about today's service. Tomorrow the kitchen opens with a different reality, a different delivery and probably different people. A turn-off that crosses closing arrives the next day without anyone having decided it, and the first person to find out is the customer who orders the product and gets turned down.

If it is going to be missing tomorrow too, the right answer is not a longer turn-off: it is taking the product out of the menu.
</Info>

### Changing the expiry without starting over

If the turn-off falls short, the three-dot menu offers **Change expiry**. It moves the time and nothing else.

<Warning>
**The reason cannot be changed.** **Change expiry** only moves the time. The reason is the evidence of why the product was turned off, and rewriting it while the turn-off is still live would leave a record that does not match what happened. If the reason genuinely changed, restore the product and turn it off again.
</Warning>

## Times are on your clock; the closing time is the store's

Two different questions, and it pays not to mix them up.

**How long may a turn-off last?** The store decides, through its operational close. It is the store that opens and closes.

**Which clock are the times on screen written in?** Yours. All of them — the dialog, the **Until** column, the detail and the **Audit** tab — are shown in your device's timezone, like everywhere else in the back office. You will never see two different times for the same turn-off.

If you manage stores in another country, you see both things at once. A São Paulo store that closes at 22:00, operated from Ecuador — two hours behind — shows *"the store closes at 08:00 PM"*. These are not two schedules: it is the same instant, written on the clock in front of you.

<Note>
The one place that names the store's clock is the error you get when you try to run past the close. There it does say the time is "the store's", because that is the schedule the store publishes and it is what makes the limit make sense.
</Note>

## Where the turn-off applies: channels

A product can run out in one channel and not in another. The **Where?** block decides the scope.

- **Every channel in this store** — the product stops selling across the whole store.
- **Only some channels** — you tick the affected channel and fulfillment pairs.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/out-of-stock/03-canales.png" alt="Picking specific channels inside the turn-off dialog" />
</Frame>

Each checkbox is one **channel · fulfillment type** pair: *Kiosk · Takeaway*, *Kiosk · Dine-In*, *Point of Sale · Takeaway*, *Point of Sale · Dine-In*. The channels listed are the ones that store has configured.

In the table, the **Scope** column shows either **All channels** or the specific pair, so you can see at a glance how far each turn-off reaches.

<Warning>
**Turning off too much costs sales.** If the product ran out for takeaway only but you still have some for dine-in, choosing **Every channel in this store** stops you selling where you did have stock. The broad scope is the easy one to tick and the one that leaves the most money on the table.
</Warning>

## When the product is also a side

A product is not always sold on its own: it can be an option inside a combo. Turning it off on the menu while leaving it selectable as a side is promising something the kitchen does not have.

So when the product you are about to turn off is also sold inside modifier groups, the dialog names them before you confirm:

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/out-of-stock/04-modificadores.png" alt="Notice listing the modifier groups where the product is also sold" />
</Frame>

There is nothing to decide here: the notice is there so you know the real reach of what you are turning off. On confirming, the product also becomes unavailable inside those groups.

## Going back on sale

There are two paths, and the normal one is to do nothing.

**It expires on its own.** When the time comes, the product goes back on sale with nobody intervening. That is the default behaviour and the reason the expiry is mandatory.

**Restore now.** If the problem was solved earlier, the three-dot menu offers **Restore now** and the product returns immediately.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/out-of-stock/05-apagados.png" alt="Table with turned-off products, their return time and the bulk action bar" />
</Frame>

The header carries two counters: how many products are **turned off** and how many are **expiring within the hour**. The second one is worth checking before a shift change.

The **Until** column shows **the time**, not a countdown. A countdown forces mental arithmetic and goes stale the moment you look at it.

### Turning several off at once

Ticking the checkboxes on several rows brings up **Turn off temporarily (N)** above the table. The dialog is the same one, and what you choose applies to every selected product: same expiry, same scope, same reason.

Only products that are currently selling are included. Ones already turned off are ignored, so there is no risk of overwriting an existing turn-off by selecting too broadly.

## The Audit tab

**Audit** keeps every turn-off in the store and how each one ended.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/out-of-stock/07-auditoria.png" alt="Turn-off history with product, scope, times, outcome, user and reason" />
</Frame>

| Outcome | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| **Active** | Still turned off right now |
| **Restored** | Someone put it back on sale by hand |
| **Expired** | The expiry was reached and it came back on its own |
| **Replaced by a sync** | A menu publication ended it |

Every row carries the user and the reason. It is the answer to *"why did we not sell fries between two and six yesterday?"*, and it is why the reason is mandatory.

The detail of a live turn-off opens from **View detail**:

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/out-of-stock/06-detalle.png" alt="Turn-off detail: scope, start, expiry, user, origin and reason" />
</Frame>

The **Via** field says where the product was turned off from. **Fire** is the back office; a turn-off made at the point of sale shows its own origin, and is restored from here just the same.

## Recipes: how real cases get solved

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The kitchen ran out of fries mid-lunch">
    1. Pick the store in the selector at the top.
    2. Type "fries" in the search box.
    3. Open the three-dot menu on the row and choose **Turn off temporarily**.
    4. Expiry **1 hour** — roughly how long the delivery takes — and scope **Every channel in this store**.
    5. Reason: *"Ran out of stock in the kitchen"*.
    6. **Turn off**.

    If the delivery arrives sooner, use **Restore now**. If it takes longer, **Change expiry**.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Turning twelve products off at once when the hot kitchen closes">
    1. Filter or search so the table shows only that station's products.
    2. Tick the checkbox on all of them.
    3. **Turn off temporarily (12)** appears above the table.
    4. Expiry **Until closing**: the hot kitchen is not reopening today.
    5. Reason: *"Hot kitchen closed for the day"*.
    6. **Turn off**.

    All twelve are turned off with the same expiry and reason, and tomorrow they wake up selling.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="It ran out at the kiosk, but there is still some at the till">
    1. Open **Turn off temporarily** on the product.
    2. Under **Where?**, choose **Only some channels**.
    3. Tick only the kiosk pairs: *Kiosk · Takeaway* and *Kiosk · Dine-In*.
    4. Reason, then **Turn off**.

    In the table, the **Scope** column stops saying "All channels" and shows the specific pair. The product keeps selling at the till.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="No delivery today: let it come back tomorrow by itself">
    1. Open **Turn off temporarily**.
    2. Expiry **Until closing**.
    3. Reason: *"No supplier delivery, restocking tomorrow"*.
    4. **Turn off**.

    The turn-off dies with the business day. When you open tomorrow the product is selling again, so if the delivery never came you have to turn it off once more — nobody will remind you.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="It was solved sooner than expected">
    1. Filter by **Turned off** to see only what is off sale.
    2. Open the product's three-dot menu.
    3. **Restore now**.

    It goes back on sale immediately. In **Audit** it is recorded as **Restored**, with the real time it came back.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The supplier is late and the turn-off falls short">
    1. Open the three-dot menu on the turned-off product.
    2. **Change expiry**.
    3. Pick the new time and save.

    Only the time moves: the original reason stays as it was. If what changed is the reason — it is no longer "out of stock" but "fryer broken" — restore the product and turn it off again with the right reason.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## One service, hour by hour

A store that closes at 10:00 PM. This is what the operator sees at each point:

| Time | What happens | Status in store | **Until** column |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 12:40 PM | Fries run out. Turned off for **1 hour**, every channel | **Turned off** | 1:40 PM |
| 1:10 PM | The delivery is late. **Change expiry** to 3:00 PM | **Turned off** | 3:00 PM |
| 2:20 PM | The delivery arrives. **Restore now** | **Selling** | — |
| 7:00 PM | They run out again, no more deliveries. **Until closing** | **Turned off** | 10:00 PM |
| 10:00 PM | The business day closes. The turn-off expires on its own | **Selling** | — |

Both entries land in **Audit**: the first as **Restored** at 2:20 PM, the second as **Expired** at 10:00 PM. Nobody had to remember anything.

And if at 7:00 PM someone had tried to turn the product off "until midday tomorrow", the dialog would not have allowed it: that store's ceiling is 10:00 PM.

## Expensive mistakes

<Warning>
**Turning off every channel when it was missing in only one.** It is the option selected by default and the quickest to confirm. If the product only ran out at the kiosk, you also stop selling it at the till and in the dining room, where you did have stock.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**A generic reason.** *"Test"*, *"no stock"*, *"xx"*. The reason is the only thing left in the history and it **cannot be edited**. When someone asks a month from now why that product was off sale for four hours, that text is the entire answer they will get.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**Assuming you can turn something off until tomorrow.** The turn-off always dies with the business day. If the product will still be missing tomorrow, you have to turn it off again when you open — or take it out of the menu, which is the right tool when the problem is not about a single service.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**Reading a time out over the phone when you are in a different country from the store.** What the screen shows is written on your clock, not the store's. Tell the manager "it comes back at eight" while they are two hours ahead and each of you is talking about a different moment.
</Warning>

## Glossary

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Temporary turn-off** | Taking a product off sale in one store, for a while and with an expiry. It does not touch the menu. |
| **Expiry** | What time the turn-off ends. Chosen when turning off, and movable afterwards. |
| **Business day** | The store's working day, with its closing time. It is the ceiling for any turn-off. |
| **Scope** | Where the turn-off applies: the whole store, or only certain channels. |
| **Channel** | How the sale comes in: kiosk, point of sale, and whichever ones the store has configured. |
| **Fulfillment type** | How the order is handed over: takeaway, dine-in. It pairs with the channel. |
| **Reason** | Why it was turned off. Mandatory, at least five characters, not editable afterwards. |
| **Modifier group** | The set of options inside a combo. A turned-off product cannot be picked there either. |
| **Audit** | The history of every turn-off in the store and how each one ended. |
| **Restore** | Putting the product back on sale before the expiry is reached. |

## Frequently asked questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Why can I not turn a product off until tomorrow?">
    Because a turn-off cannot outlive the store's closing time. It is a rule, not a technical limit: the state of today's kitchen says nothing about tomorrow's service, and a turn-off that crosses the night arrives the next day without anyone having decided it.

    If the product will be missing for several days, the right move is to take it out of the menu.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Does the turn-off reach the sales channels?">
    Today the turn-off lives in Fire: the store and the operation see it, and it is recorded in **Audit**. Propagating it to the sales channels is the next stage and is listed under **What is coming**.

    In the meantime, treat this screen as what it is: the operational record of what stopped being sold, when, and why.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Which timezone are the times I see in?">
    Your device's, on every screen: the dialog, the **Until** column, the detail and the **Audit** tab all use the same clock, so you will never see two different times for the same turn-off.

    What does not depend on your clock is the limit. A turn-off cannot run past the store's closing time, and that closing time is the store's; if you are in another country, you see it translated into your own.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What happens if nobody restores the product?">
    It goes back on sale by itself when the expiry is reached. That is the whole point: a turn-off does not need anyone to remember to undo it. In **Audit** it is recorded as **Expired**.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I am looking for a product and it is not in the list">
    The list shows what that store actually sells: the products in the menu it has published or assigned. If the product is not in that menu, it cannot be turned off because it is not being sold there.

    Check which menu the product belongs to and which menu the store has assigned.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The store says it has no menu assigned">
    This appears when the store has no menu yet, so there is nothing to turn off. The **Go to menu assignments** button takes you straight to fixing it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I restore a turn-off made at the point of sale?">
    Yes. A live turn-off is operational state, not the property of whoever created it. The **Via** field in the detail says where it was made, but it does not restrict who can put the product back on sale.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## What is coming

- **Notifying the sales channels.** Having the turn-off travel automatically to the channels where the product is sold, so it stops being offered there too.
- **Automatic closing on menu publication.** Today, if you republish a store's menu while products are turned off, those turn-offs still show on this screen. The **Replaced by a sync** outcome exists for this and is not applied automatically yet.
- **Hot pricing.** Changing a product's price during service with the same expiry-and-reason mechanics, without touching the price list.
