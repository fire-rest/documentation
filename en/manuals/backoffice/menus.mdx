title: "Menus"
description: "Build the menu for every channel out of a single one, without maintaining five versions of the same product in parallel."
---

A menu answers one question: *what do we sell?* What changes is **where**. The dine-in menu is not the delivery menu, the airport store is not the downtown one, and at two in the morning you don't sell what you sell at noon. Keeping five menus in parallel, product by product, is where the mistakes creep in — the ones someone finds later on a ticket.

This screen exists so you can have many different menus **without maintaining them five times**. Go to **Restaurant OS → Menu and Products → Menus**.

---

## The minimum you need to know

<Note>
**1. You already have a menu, even if you never created one.** It's the **Golden**, and it holds your whole catalog. It is created on its own when you add products, and it cannot be deleted.

**2. A custom menu inherits from its base menu and only stores the differences.** It is not a copy: if you add a product to the catalog tomorrow, the custom menu has it too. The only thing it stores is where it departs.

**3. A menu has no fulfillment type of its own.** Delivery, pickup or dine-in is decided by the row it is assigned to in [Menu assignment](/en/manuals/backoffice/menu-assignments). The same menu can serve several types, and inside it you can adjust a few things per type.
</Note>

---

## The simple path

You don't need to create any menu to start selling.

You load your products into the catalog and the **Golden** builds itself out of all of them. That is your menu, and it serves every channel until a reason appears for it not to.

The Golden carries the **Golden** label and cannot be deleted: it is the entire catalog, the floor everything else hangs from.

<Tip>
If you sell the same thing everywhere, this is all you need. The rest of the manual is for when the second menu shows up.
</Tip>

## Golden and custom: two ways to have a menu

| | Golden | Custom |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How it is born | On its own, when you add products to the catalog | You create it |
| What it holds | The whole catalog | What it inherits from its base menu, plus your differences |
| Fulfillment type | None of its own: the assignment row decides | The same: none of its own |
| Can be deleted | No | Yes, unless another menu inherits from it |
| **Overrides** tab | Does not have one | Yes, when it inherits from another menu: it's where your changes live |

<Info>
**Why "inherits" and not "copies".** A copy freezes on the day you make it: three months later it is missing the twenty products that entered the catalog since, and nobody notices until a customer asks. A custom menu doesn't have that problem because it doesn't store products, it stores **differences**. Whatever you didn't change still comes from the base menu, always current.

It is the same logic as [linked price lists](/en/manuals/backoffice/price-lists), applied to structure instead of price.
</Info>

## Creating a custom menu

**Create Custom Menu**, top right. Three fields, and only one of them needs thought.

| Field | What to put in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Menu name** *(required)* | The name you'll search for six months from now: *Late Night*, *Airport*, *Breakfast*. |
| **Base menu** | Who it inherits from. See below. |
| **Description** *(optional)* | What it's for. *"Active from 22:00 to 06:00 for delivery in urban areas"*. |

There is no fulfillment type field: you don't choose it here, and you don't need to get it right on day one. That's decided later, row by row, when you assign the menu.

### The field that matters: Base menu

Who it inherits from decides how much work you'll have from here on.

| Option | What it means | When to use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Golden Menu** *(default)* | Starts with the whole catalog; you only store what you remove or change. | Almost always. |
| **No parent (empty menu)** | An empty menu. You add categories and products by hand. | A new brand that shares nothing with the current catalog. |
| **Or base it on another custom menu** | Inherits the categories and products of a custom menu you already built — **but not its changes**. See the warning below. | When you need a variant of a menu's contents, not of the whole catalog. |

<Warning>
**A menu based on another custom menu inherits its structure, not its decisions.** What you removed, hid, re-photographed or set up differently in the questions of the base menu stays in that menu. The new one starts from the base menu's categories and products as if none of those changes existed: the products you removed there show up again here.

If what you need is "the delivery menu, minus two products", you'll have to redo the delivery menu's changes on the new one.
</Warning>

<Note>
**Inheritance goes three levels deep**: Golden → custom → custom. There is no fourth. The selector only offers menus that can still be parents; if a menu isn't in the list, it's because hanging something off it would cross the limit. (The hint under the field still says nested custom menus aren't supported; they are, up to that third level.)
</Note>

Confirming with **Create and edit menu** creates the menu and drops you straight into the editor.

## The fulfillment type comes from the row, not from the menu

A menu doesn't belong to delivery or to dine-in. **Any menu can be assigned to any row** of [Menu assignment](/en/manuals/backoffice/menu-assignments) — store × channel × fulfillment type — and it's that row that decides which type the channel receives.

That opens two ways of working, and both are valid:

| | One menu with per-type adjustments | One menu per type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What you do | Assign the same menu to the delivery and dine-in rows, and use the **Fulfillment type** lens to adjust what changes | Create a custom menu for delivery and another for dine-in |
| What you can make different | Which products are shown, the order, the product photos and the questions (min, max, options) | Everything: which products exist at all, the categories, the names coming from the catalog |
| When it fits | The catalog is the same and only the presentation changes: delivery shows the combos first, with another photo | Delivery really sells something else: fewer products, no dishes that travel badly |

<Info>
**What the per-type adjustments cannot do: remove a product for one type only.** **Remove from menu** always removes it from the whole menu, whatever type you are looking at. To take a product off delivery and keep it for dine-in, either hide it for that type or use a separate menu. Both are explained below.
</Info>

## What the list tells you

The list is a **tree**: custom menus hang off their parent and fold and unfold. That way you see at a glance who inherits from whom.

| Column | What to look at |
| --- | --- |
| **Name** | The name, the description, and who it inherits from (*Based on*). |
| **Type** | **Golden** or **Custom**. |
| **Products** · **Categories** | How many it has, inherited ones included. |
| **Overrides** | How many differences you stored against the parent. **—** if you changed nothing. |
| **Status** | **Published** · **Partial** · **Draft** · **Archived**, with a colored dot. |
| **Last publish** | When it last reached a channel. |

Two notices can appear under the status: **sync pending** (it was sent and the channel hasn't confirmed) and **sync failed** (the channel rejected it). Both are resolved on the [Menu assignment](/en/manuals/backoffice/menu-assignments) screen, not here.

<Warning>
**Today, Status and Last publish don't move when a menu is synced.** Every menu shows **Draft** and **—**, including the ones already selling, and the editor repeats it: *"This menu is a draft and is not visible to customers."* Don't read it as "nobody uses this menu". What is live, where, is on [Menu assignment](/en/manuals/backoffice/menu-assignments), row by row.
</Warning>

At the top you have search by name and two filters: type and status.

The **Delete** action only appears on custom menus that no other menu inherits from. To delete a parent, delete its children first.

<Note>
**If you're looking for the assignments list here, it's gone.** It used to live in a tab on this screen and moved to [Menu assignment](/en/manuals/backoffice/menu-assignments), which is a screen of its own and does considerably more.
</Note>

## Building the menu: the Structure tab

The editor has three tabs — **Structure**, **Overrides** and **History** — and Structure is where the work happens.

- **Add category** and **Add** *(inside each category)* open a catalog search — they offer what already exists in [Products](/en/manuals/backoffice/products) and [Categories](/en/manuals/backoffice/categories). What you add goes into this menu and touches nobody else.
- **You reorder by dragging.** Categories among themselves, and products inside each category. The order you leave is the order the customer sees.
- Two views of the same menu: **Visual**, to arrange things and see how they look, and **Table**, to review many products in a row with their prices and their badges.

---

## Saving is all you have to do

This is the part that surprises everyone: **there is no publish button in the editor**.

The menu keeps its structure current **just by saving**. What used to be publish, sync and preview buttons inside the editor moved entirely to the assignments screen, because publishing is not a property of the menu: it's a decision about which store and which channel uses it.

While you have unsaved changes you'll see the amber **Unsaved changes** chip. **Save draft** turns it into **Draft saved**.

If you try to leave with changes pending — another tab of the editor, a link, the breadcrumb, another account, closing the browser tab — the screen stops you with **You have unsaved changes**. It lists what's pending under **Draft changes** (prices, visibility, modifier groups, images, order and contents) and offers three ways out: **Stay here**, **Discard changes** or **Save and continue**.

## Removing is not the same as hiding

Two actions that look alike and do different things. The difference shows up when you want to undo.

| | **Remove from menu** | **Hide** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it does | Takes the product out of the menu entirely | Leaves it in the menu, but not shown |
| Per fulfillment type | No: always for the whole menu | Yes, with the lens on a type: **Hide for Delivery** |
| Where it goes | The **Removed** tab of the add dialog | The **Hidden** tab |
| How it comes back | **Restore** | **Show** |
| When to use it | The product doesn't belong on this menu, full stop | It's off sale for a while, or for one type only, but it's still part of the menu |

The interface says it itself: *"Removed products are taken out of this menu entirely. To keep a product but not show it, use Hide instead."*

<Warning>
**None of this is for "we ran out mid-service".** Removing or hiding a product here is a menu decision, permanent until somebody undoes it by hand. If what happened is that the fries ran out at two in the afternoon, that belongs in [Out of stock](/en/manuals/backoffice/out-of-stock), which comes back on its own and doesn't rely on anyone remembering.
</Warning>

## What you can change for this menu only

A menu can depart from its base in several things at once. Every field you change is marked with a blue **Override** chip, and the differences show up together on the **Overrides** tab.

| What you can change | On | Can it differ per fulfillment type? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Visibility** *(Hide / Show)* | Product and category | Products yes; categories no |
| **Image** — *"Only applies to this menu. The base image stays untouched."* | Product and category | Products yes; categories no |
| **Order** *(dragging)* | Categories and products | Yes, both — dragging in the **Visual** view |
| **Modifiers** — group name, **Min** and **Max**, which options are offered | Product | Yes |
| **Remove from menu** | Product and category | No: always the whole menu |

Single or multiple choice is not set by hand: it follows the maximum. A group with **Max** 1 is **Single**; raise it to 3 and it becomes **Multiple** on its own.

**Upselling** and **cross-selling** suggestions can be seen from the product row, but they are read-only: today they are always inherited from the product and cannot be changed per menu.

### The Overrides tab

The **Overrides** tab opens with *"Inherits from Golden Menu — this tab shows only the differences."* and splits into three blocks: **Product differences**, **Removed products** and **Category overrides**. Each row has **Reset**, and **Reset all** sits at the top: they return that product — or all of them — to the parent's values.

<Info>
**The Price chip you'll see there is not a menu change.** It is the difference between what the product costs on the price list you are viewing the menu through and its catalog price, plus the count of modifier option prices fixed on that list. It shows up alongside the rest so you can see every difference in one place, but it is corrected on the [price list](/en/manuals/backoffice/price-lists), not on the menu.
</Info>

<Note>
**Two things the Overrides tab doesn't show.** It only exists on menus that inherit from another one: the Golden and menus born with **No parent (empty menu)** have nothing to compare against. And it doesn't list per-fulfillment-type adjustments: to find those, use the dots in the **Fulfillment type** selector and the violet chips in **Structure**, explained below.
</Note>

## The three lenses: day, fulfillment type and prices

Three selectors above the editor. Two of them change only **what you see**; the third — the fulfillment type — also decides **where what you change gets written**, and that's the one people misread.

### Day — the day lens

**Day** and **Time** filter the view to show you how the menu looks on that day at that hour. Categories with their own schedule that doesn't cover that moment disappear, and the count of how many are *hidden by schedule* appears at the top.

It is **only a filter**: it doesn't edit schedules and doesn't change the menu. Schedules are set on categories and products, not here.

### Fulfillment type — the lens that also writes

The **Fulfillment type** selector starts on **All fulfillment types**: that is the menu's **base**, what every type gets unless it has something of its own. Pick a type — Delivery, say — and the editor shows you the menu as that type's rows will receive it: its visibility, its order, its photos, its questions, and the prices that type has on the list you are viewing.

The important part: **while a type is selected, everything you change applies only to that type.** The product eye reads **Hide for Delivery**, dragging writes the delivery order, the photo dialog warns *"You're editing the Delivery image for this menu. The other types keep the shared one."*, and the question controls write the delivery minimum and maximum. To change the base, go back to **All fulfillment types**.

How you tell what is the type's own and what it inherits:

- Next to the selector, a badge says **custom** if that type has something of its own in this menu, or **inherited** if it follows the base. In the list of types, a dot marks the ones that already have their own adjustments, so you don't have to check them one by one.
- With the lens off, a violet chip on the product row — **2 type(s)** — warns that the product isn't present the same way in every type, and a question shows **differs in 2**.
- With the lens on, a question set up for that type shows **own to this type**, and a reordered category shows **product order for Delivery**.

Every adjustment has its way back to the base: **Back to the base** on questions, **back to the base order** on order, **follow the base price** on prices, and **Remove override** in the photo dialog.

<Info>
**Hiding for one type doesn't erase the product from that channel.** It reaches that type's rows switched off, so it can't be ordered there, and it stays in the menu for the rest. A product hidden in the base can also be shown for one single type: the lens can turn on what the base turned off.
</Info>

Two details that save scares:

- **Switching the fulfillment type doesn't discard anything.** What you did under Delivery is already addressed to Delivery; you can move to another type and save everything together.
- **The lens doesn't survive a page reload.** If you save under Delivery and reload, the editor comes back on **All fulfillment types** and it looks as if your changes were lost. They weren't: pick Delivery again.

Dragging under a type only works in the **Visual** view: **Table** shows that type's order but doesn't let you change it.

### Prices from — the price lens

It shows the prices from whichever list you pick, and lets you edit them right there without leaving the menu. With nothing picked, it shows the list the menu uses by default, marked **Menu price list** in the selector. Useful for reviewing the menu through the channel's eyes: *"this is how the delivery menu looks with the delivery list"*.

If the list you picked is linked to another one, each cell shows its badge — **Formula** or **Fixed** — with the count of each at the top.

Combined with the fulfillment type lens, the price cell edits **that type's price on that list**: it gets the **own** chip, and **follow the base price** takes it back to the list's base price.

**Sale prices show up, but they aren't edited here.** When a product has a sale price on the list, the regular price appears struck through and the sale shows below it in green — *sale BRL 7,90* — with the hint *"Sale price from the price list (edit it there)"*. A combo does the same with its calculated reference (**calc. ref.**): struck through, with the price it comes to once its options' sale prices are applied. To change a sale price, go to the [price list](/en/manuals/backoffice/price-lists).

<Warning>
**Switching price lists with unsaved prices discards them.** The *Unsaved prices* warning appears and the only way out is **Discard and switch**. Save before changing lists.
</Warning>

<Info>
**What the lens does not do: it does not change the menu's list.** Looking at another list's prices reassigns nothing. Which list each store uses is decided in [Menu assignment](/en/manuals/backoffice/menu-assignments).
</Info>

## Price does not live in the menu

<Info>
**A menu says what is sold and in what order. Never how much it costs.** Price lives in the [price lists](/en/manuals/backoffice/price-lists), and the one that applies comes from the list assigned to that store, that channel and that fulfillment type. When you edit a price from the menu editor, you are writing on the list you're viewing — never on the menu.

That's why two stores can use exactly the same menu and charge differently: same menu, different list. It's why raising a price requires touching no menu at all. And it's why a sale price is set on the list, even though the editor shows it.
</Info>

---

## A menu doesn't sell until it's assigned

You finished building the menu, you saved, and nothing happened. That's correct: **saving does not publish**.

A menu only goes on sale when somebody says which store, which channel and which fulfillment type it's used for, and presses **Sync**. That happens in [Menu assignment](/en/manuals/backoffice/menu-assignments), which is also where the **sync pending** and **sync failed** notices you see in the list get resolved. Any menu can go on any row: the row's fulfillment type is the one that applies, along with the adjustments you made for that type.

## Recipes: how the real cases get solved

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Building the late-night menu">
    After 22:00 the kitchen runs on half a brigade and can't sustain the whole menu.

    1. **Create Custom Menu**. Name: *Late Night*.
    2. **Base menu**: **Golden Menu** — it starts with everything and you remove what doesn't fit.
    3. In **Structure**, use **Remove from menu** on everything the kitchen can't make at night.
    4. **Save draft**.
    5. Go to **Menu assignment** and assign it to the rows of the stores that open late — delivery, pickup or both: the same menu serves them all.

    The products that stayed keep inheriting from the Golden: if one of their product records changes tomorrow, the late-night menu finds out on its own.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Opening an airport store with a reduced menu and higher prices">
    Two different things, solved on two different screens.

    1. **The menu**: create a custom menu inheriting from the Golden and remove what doesn't sell at an airport.
    2. **The prices**: create a [price list](/en/manuals/backoffice/price-lists) linked to the base one, with `=P*1.15`.
    3. In **Menu assignment**, give the airport store **that menu and that list**.

    You don't need to touch prices inside the menu. They are independent axes on purpose: the same menu can live alongside five different lists.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Stopping a product on delivery without touching dine-in">
    The schnitzels arrive cold and they decided to stop selling them for delivery. There are two ways, depending on how your menus are set up.

    **If delivery and dine-in use the same menu:**

    1. Open that menu and, in **Fulfillment type**, pick **Delivery**.
    2. Find the product and use **Hide for Delivery**.
    3. **Save draft**.
    4. In **Menu assignment**, sync the delivery rows using that menu.

    Dine-in keeps it as it was. On delivery the product reaches the channel switched off.

    **If delivery has its own menu:** open it, use **Remove from menu** on the product, save and sync. If you change your mind later, it's in the **Removed** tab of the add dialog, with **Restore**.

    Don't use **Remove from menu** in the first case: it removes the product from the whole menu, and dine-in loses it too.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Putting the combos first, with another photo, on delivery only">
    On delivery you want *Combo para 2* to be the first thing the customer sees, with a photo of the packaged order; in the dining room, the menu stays as it is.

    1. Open the menu the delivery and dine-in rows use, and pick **Delivery** in **Fulfillment type**.
    2. In the **Visual** view, drag *Combo para 2* to the top of its category. The category shows **product order for Delivery**.
    3. Click the product's photo and upload the delivery one. The dialog confirms that the other types keep the shared photo.
    4. **Save draft** and sync the delivery rows in **Menu assignment**.

    To undo it, with the lens on Delivery: **back to the base order** for the order, and **Remove override** in the photo dialog.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Opening a new brand that shares nothing with the current one">
    1. **Create Custom Menu** with **Base menu → No parent (empty menu)**.
    2. Add categories with **Add category** and products with **Add**.
    3. Arrange the order by dragging.
    4. **Save draft** and assign it on the other screen.

    This menu inherits from nobody: whatever enters the catalog tomorrow will **not** show up here on its own. That's the flip side of starting empty, and the reason to pick this option only when there really is nothing to share.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Preparing the menu for an aggregator">
    An aggregator usually wants fewer products and its own photos.

    1. Create a custom menu inheriting from the **Golden**.
    2. Remove the products that don't belong on that channel.
    3. Where a different photo is needed, change it: it gets the **Override** chip and **does not touch the original product record**.
    4. Prices go separately: a list linked to the base one that offsets the commission.
    5. Assign menu and list to that channel's rows, and sync.

    Everything you didn't change keeps inheriting. The day you upload a new photo to the catalog, this menu picks it up on its own — except on the products where you set one of your own.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Mistakes that cost money

<Warning>
**Changing a menu with the lens on a type when you meant all of them — or the other way round.** With **Delivery** selected, what you hide, reorder or re-photograph only applies to delivery; the dining room keeps seeing it. With **All fulfillment types**, the change reaches every type that doesn't have its own. Look at the selector before you start.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**Using Remove from menu to take a product off a single fulfillment type.** It removes it from the whole menu, and every row using that menu loses it — dine-in included. For one type, use **Hide for** that type, or give that type its own menu.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**Basing a menu on another custom menu and expecting its changes to come along.** Only the categories and products come along. What the base menu removed, hid or set up differently is not inherited: the removed products show up again on the new menu, and they reach the channel at the next sync if nobody reviews it.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**Using Remove from menu for a product that ran out today.** Removing it is a menu decision and it stays that way until someone restores it by hand. For the temporary case there's [Out of stock](/en/manuals/backoffice/out-of-stock), which comes back on its own.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**Believing that saving the menu publishes it.** Saving keeps the structure current; the channel learns nothing until somebody syncs in [Menu assignment](/en/manuals/backoffice/menu-assignments).
</Warning>

<Warning>
**Starting with No parent (empty menu) out of habit.** A menu without a parent inherits nothing: new catalog products will never appear on their own, and six months later the menu is incomplete with nothing to warn you.
</Warning>

## Glossary

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Golden** | The catalog's menu. It is created on its own, holds everything, and is not deleted. |
| **Custom** | A menu that inherits from another one and stores only the differences. |
| **Base menu** | Who a custom menu inherits from. Chosen at creation. It passes on its categories and products, not its changes. |
| **Fulfillment type** | Delivery, pickup, dine-in… A menu doesn't have one: the assignment row decides it. |
| **Row** | Store × channel × fulfillment type, on [Menu assignment](/en/manuals/backoffice/menu-assignments). It's the row that gets a menu and a price list. |
| **Base** | What every fulfillment type gets unless it has something of its own. It's what you edit with **All fulfillment types** selected. |
| **Per-type adjustment** | Visibility, order, photo or question settings that apply to one fulfillment type only. Marked **custom** / **own**; undone with **Back to the base**. |
| **Override** | A field that is worth something different on this menu than on the parent. Marked with a blue chip. |
| **Reset** | Returning an override to the parent's value. |
| **Removed** | A product taken out of the menu entirely. Brought back with **Restore**. |
| **Hidden** | A product still on the menu but not shown. Brought back with **Show**. |
| **Lens** | A selector above the editor: the day, the price list or the fulfillment type. The first two only change what you see; the fulfillment type also decides which type your changes go to. |

## Frequently asked questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="I saved the menu and nothing changed in the app">
    Saving does not publish. The channel receives the menu only when somebody syncs the corresponding row in [Menu assignment](/en/manuals/backoffice/menu-assignments).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Where do I choose the menu's fulfillment type?">
    Nowhere on this screen: menus don't have one. The fulfillment type belongs to the row in [Menu assignment](/en/manuals/backoffice/menu-assignments). If a menu was set up thinking of pickup and you need it for delivery, assign it to the delivery rows; nothing has to be rebuilt.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can the same menu serve delivery and dine-in?">
    Yes. Assign it to both rows, and use the **Fulfillment type** lens for what has to differ: what is shown, the order, the photos and the questions. If delivery really sells other products, a separate menu is clearer.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I saved changes for Delivery, reloaded the page, and they're gone">
    They aren't gone: the lens goes back to **All fulfillment types** on every reload, and there you see the base. Pick **Delivery** again and your changes are there.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The menu says Draft, but it's already selling">
    That's expected today: the menu's status and last publish don't update when its rows are synced. What is live, and where, is on [Menu assignment](/en/manuals/backoffice/menu-assignments).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I added a product to the catalog and it isn't on my custom menu">
    Check who that menu inherits from. If it was born with **No parent (empty menu)**, it inherits from nobody and new products have to be added by hand with **Add**. If it inherits from the Golden, it should appear on its own; if it isn't there, check whether somebody removed it with **Remove from menu** — it will be in the **Removed** tab, with **Restore**.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Why can't I delete the Golden menu?">
    Because it is the catalog. It's where the products you load land, and the starting point the other menus inherit from. Deleting it would leave the custom menus inheriting from nothing.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## What's coming

What does not exist today, so nobody promises it:

- **The History tab**, empty today. The banner already hints at the scope: publish, override, structure and assignment events, with the full audit planned for a second stage.
- **Duplicating a menu** and **viewing its JSON** — the actions are in the editor menu, still disabled.
- **Menu schedules** from this screen: today the day lens only filters, and schedules are set on categories and products.
- **Per-menu suggestions**: today upselling and cross-selling are always inherited from the product and can't be changed for one menu in particular.
- **Per-type product names, category photos or category visibility**: per fulfillment type, a product can change its visibility, order, photo and questions; a category, only its order.
- **A per-menu status that follows the syncs**: today **Status** and **Last publish** in the list don't reflect what is live.
