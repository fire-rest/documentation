The product is the piece everything else hangs off. A menu invents nothing: it just picks products from the catalog and orders them. A price list creates nothing: it puts a price on products that already exist. Neither does a modifier group: its options **are** products.

That's why this screen is the starting point. Go to **Restaurant OS → Menu and Products → Products**.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/products/01-listado.png" alt="Product catalog list" />
</Frame>

## The minimum you need to know

<Note>
**1. The type is chosen when you create the product.** Product, Combo, Modifier or Packaging. The first three can be swapped later from the record's header; **Packaging is final**.

**2. One product can be named, shown and filed differently per fulfillment type, with no duplicates.** You set it once on the record, with the **Fulfillment type** bar, and it applies to every menu. Whatever you don't change is inherited.

**3. Creating or editing the product does not put it on sale.** It has to be in a menu, and the rows of that menu have to be synced — again after every change to the record.
</Note>

---

## The simple path

**New product**, name and code, save. With that the product exists and enters the Golden Menu.

Then, on the **Pricing** tab, you give it a price. And it can be sold as soon as the menu holding it is synced.

<Tip>
If you sell standalone dishes with no customization, the **General** and **Pricing** tabs are all you need. The rest of the manual is for combos, modifiers and prices by fulfillment type.
</Tip>

## The four product types

The **New Product** button creates a product directly; the caret next to it offers the other three types. The difference matters from day one.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/products/02-tipos.png" alt="Type selector when creating a product" />
</Frame>

| Type | What it is | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Product** | An item sold directly. | A burger, a soft drink. |
| **Combo** | A bundle made up of several products. | *2 Strips + Fries + Drink*. |
| **Modifier** | An option that modifies another product. | *Extra cheese*, *Large size*. |
| **Packaging** | The container associated with a product. | A box, a cup. |

In the record's header there's a selector with three pills — **Modifier**, **Product**, **Combo** — to move between them later. **Packaging** isn't there: that one is final, and if you get it wrong you have to create the product again.

<Warning>
**Changing type isn't free.** Switching to **Combo** **deletes the prices** the product had. Switching to **Modifier** **removes its modifier groups**, because a modifier is an answer and can't have questions hanging off it; the screen asks you before doing it.

The change stays pending — with the **Unsaved** mark — until you save.
</Warning>

## The product record

The editor is split into tabs, and several are locked until you save for the first time: *"Save the product first to access this section"*.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/products/03-general.png" alt="General tab of the product record" />
</Frame>

| Tab | What for |
| --- | --- |
| **General** | Names in three languages, codes, classification, and the status, visibility and schedule switches. |
| **Pricing** | Gross price, tax, suggested price, prices by fulfillment type and additional currencies. |
| **Categories** | Which categories it's in. It can be in several, and each fulfillment type can have its own list. |
| **Images** | The photos, with the option of assigning them per fulfillment type. |
| **Modifier Groups** | The questions the customer is asked when ordering it. |
| **Suggestions** | Upselling and cross-selling, with a list of their own per fulfillment type. |

Under the title, above the tabs, sits the **Fulfillment type** bar. It's what lets you edit the record for one fulfillment type at a time: see [One product, different per fulfillment type](#one-product-different-per-fulfillment-type).

### Not every tab is always there

Which ones you see depends on the type, and that says a good deal about what each type is:

| Type | Tabs |
| --- | --- |
| **Product** | General · Pricing · Categories · Images · Modifier Groups · Suggestions |
| **Combo** | General · Categories · Images · **Composition** · Suggestions |
| **Modifier** | General · Pricing · Categories · Images |
| **Packaging** | General · Pricing · Categories · Images · Modifier Groups |

Three things that table tells you:

- **A combo has no pricing tab.** It isn't an oversight: a combo has no price of its own. What the customer pays is the sum of the options they pick, priced on the destination's price list. Its suggested price is typed on **Composition**.
- **Composition is the Modifier Groups tab, renamed.** A combo is built with the same mechanics of groups and options: each group is a decision — *pick your drink* — and the minimum says how many are included.
- **A Modifier has neither groups nor suggestions.** It's an answer, not a question, and it isn't sold as its own order line.

### The two descriptions

As with categories, there's an **operational description** — internal, for your team — and a **marketing description**, which is the one that gets published and read by the customer. Don't mix them up.

The marketing one has a **Generate with AI** button: it proposes three versions per language and **applies nothing until you choose**. The screen says so explicitly.

Both descriptions, like the name, can vary per fulfillment type, and the AI generates for the type you have chosen on the bar. See [Name and descriptions per type](#name-and-descriptions-per-type).

## One product, different per fulfillment type

The same product doesn't have to be called, shown or filed the same way in every fulfillment type. *Combo Crunch Salad* can be «Combo Crunch Salad DIN» in dine-in, and *Batata Grande* can sit in a different category there. You don't duplicate the product: you load the variation once on the record and every menu uses it.

### The fulfillment type bar

Under the title, on every tab, sits the **Fulfillment type** bar. The selector offers **All fulfillment types** — the base, what the product is — and then each type (**Delivery**, **Dine-In**, **Takeaway**…).

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/products/06-lente-barra.png" alt="Fulfillment type bar on All fulfillment types, with one dot per type" />
</Frame>

Choosing a type puts the record under the lens of that type: the bar changes color and spells it out — *"Editing Dine-In: changes apply only to Dine-In"*. Next to the selector a chip says whether that type has its own configuration (**custom**) or follows the base (**inherited**).

On the right of the bar there's one **dot per fulfillment type**:

- **Filled**: that type has its own configuration, **saved**.
- **Hollow**: it inherits everything from the base.

A change you haven't saved doesn't light the dot. And the dot uses the brand color (red/pink): **it is not an alert**.

Each field carries its own chip:

| Chip | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **custom** | The value belongs to this type. Underneath you see *"Base: …"* and the **revert to inherited** link. |
| **inherited** | Same as the base: *"Same as in All fulfillment types"*. |
| **shared** | It doesn't vary by fulfillment type. |
| **unsaved** | You changed it and haven't saved yet. |

**Inheritance works field by field and language by language.** What you don't change in a type comes from **All fulfillment types**. **revert to inherited** drops the type's own value and goes back to following the base; like any other change, it counts once you save.

### What varies and what doesn't

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/products/09-candado.png" alt="Dine-In card with the shared fields and the Does not vary by fulfillment type notice" />
</Frame>

| Can be different per fulfillment type | Same in all of them |
| --- | --- |
| Name, operational description and marketing description (per language) | External ID, Code and Recipe ID |
| Visibility | Tags |
| Category list | **Channel Exclusive Product** |
| Suggestions (upselling and cross-selling) | Catalog status, availability and sales limits |
| Schedule | Product type (the **Modifier / Product / Combo** selector shows a lock) |
| Price, image and modifier questions | KDS category |

What doesn't vary shows a lock: **Does not vary by fulfillment type** — *"It is the same in every fulfillment type. To edit it, choose All fulfillment types."* The **Go to All fulfillment types** link takes you there.

### Name and descriptions per type

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/products/07-lente-general.png" alt="General tab with the lens on Dine-In: custom name, inherited operational description, custom marketing description" />
</Frame>

On the **General** tab, with a type chosen, the card reads **Names and identifiers · Dine-In**: *"Name, operations description and marketing description apply in Dine-In only. Whatever you do not change is inherited from All fulfillment types."*

You edit by language (**PT · ES · EN**), and a blue dot on a language means the type has its own text there. In the example, *Combo Crunch Salad* has the name «Combo Crunch Salad DIN» in Dine-In (**custom**, with *Base: Combo Crunch Salad* underneath), the operational description is still **inherited** and the marketing one is **custom**.

**Generate with AI** also works per type, and says which one it's filling: *"Generating for Dine-In: the text you apply fills Dine-In only, not All fulfillment types."* Nothing is applied until you choose.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/products/10-ia-por-tipo.png" alt="Generate marketing description dialog with the Generating for Dine-In notice" />
</Frame>

<Warning>
**Load the type's text in every language the base has.** The published name prefers Spanish when the base has it. If the base has ES and PT and you load the Dine-In name only in PT, Dine-In keeps going out with the base name in Spanish.
</Warning>

### Hiding a product in one type

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/products/08-visible-por-tipo.png" alt="Visible in Dine-In card with the switch on and the inherited chip" />
</Frame>

With a type chosen, the **General** tab shows the **Visible in Dine-In** card: *"Turned off, the product is hidden in Dine-In in every menu. A menu can still show it again in Dine-In."*

A type can only hide what the base shows on the record. And hiding it here isn't the last word: a specific menu can still show it again in that type.

### Categories per type

Without a type chosen, the **Categories** tab shows the base list and, underneath, the **By Fulfillment Type** summary: *"These fulfillment types use their own list instead of the base list."* **Edit** puts that type on the bar.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/products/11-categorias-resumen.png" alt="Categories tab with the base list ACOMPANHAMENTOS and the By Fulfillment Type summary" />
</Frame>

With the lens on a type, you see that type's list. If you edit it, the type gets its own list: *"Own list for Dine-In: it replaces the base list only in Dine-In."* It **replaces**, it doesn't add to the base.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/products/12-categorias-por-tipo.png" alt="Categories tab with the lens on Dine-In: own list REFEIÇÃO, base ACOMPANHAMENTOS" />
</Frame>

The product comes out in a row's category only if **its list for that type** includes that category. The Golden Menu places it in all its categories (the base ones plus those of the per-type lists), and each row publishes only those of its own type.

An empty list also counts: *"No categories in Dine-In: the product does not appear in this fulfillment type."*

### Suggestions and schedule per type

Suggestions follow the same logic as categories. With a type chosen, upselling and cross-selling start **inherited** — *"If you edit it, Delivery gets its own list."* — and if you edit them the type gets an own list that replaces the base only there. An empty list is valid: *"No suggestions in Delivery."*

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/products/13-sugeridos-por-tipo.png" alt="Suggestions tab with the lens on Delivery: upselling and cross-selling inherited" />
</Frame>

The schedule also works per type. With a type chosen it appears as **Schedule in \{type\}**: *"An own schedule replaces the whole schedule of All fulfillment types in \{type\}. Without one, \{type\} inherits it."* It replaces the **whole** schedule; it doesn't adjust a day or two of it.

<Info>
**Why it lives on the record.** It's loaded once, on the product, and applies to **every menu**. The menu editor gains no fields: under the lens of a type it shows what the record defines, and on publishing each row (store × channel × type) goes out with what its type defines. A product with no per-type configuration is published exactly as before.
</Info>

## The three switches that decide whether it sells

They look alike and do different things. This is where people get it wrong most often.

| Switch | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Product status** *(active/inactive)* | Inactive: **excluded from every menu and cannot be sold**. |
| **Show in menu** | Off: doesn't appear on the menu, **but keeps working inside combos and as a modifier option**. |
| **Out of stock** | Temporarily unavailable. May show greyed out on the menu. |

<Info>
**"Show in menu" being off is what makes modifiers possible.** An *extra cheese* product shouldn't appear on the menu as a dish, but it does have to be available when somebody customizes a burger. Turning visibility off while leaving the product active is exactly that combination.
</Info>

### The commercial state isn't editable

On the **General** tab, next to those switches, there's a **commercial state** — **Available**, **Out of stock** or **Not sellable** — that **the system calculates** by combining all of the above. The screen spells it out: *"Automatically calculated by the snapshot engine. Not editable."*

It's the traffic light for what's really going on with a product without having to check switch by switch.

### And a fourth, per fulfillment type

With a fulfillment type chosen on the bar, the **General** tab adds **Visible in \{type\}**: it hides the product in that type only. It doesn't replace any of the three above. See [Hiding a product in one type](#hiding-a-product-in-one-type).

## Channel-exclusive product

The same switch that exists on categories. By default the product **is part of the Golden Menu** and every channel and store inherits it. Turning it on takes it out of the Golden Menu, and it only exists in the menus where somebody adds it by hand.

<Note>
If its **category** is already exclusive, the product inherits that exclusivity without you marking it. See [Categories](/en/manuals/backoffice/categories).
</Note>

## Price

The **base price** is the one from the catalog's base list: *"Default price from the base price list"*. What you save here lands in that list — and from there it is the starting point for every list connected to it.

### You type what the customer pays

**Gross price** *(after taxes)* is the only price you type: it's what the customer pays. **Net price (without tax)** is read-only and calculated on its own from the gross and the tax: *"Enter the gross price, which is what the customer pays. The net price is calculated from it and the selected tax."*

<Warning>
**An empty price box is not a zero.** The tab refuses to save with the gross empty. A typed **0** is valid — and it means the product is free on every list that follows the base. Type 0 only if that's really what you mean.
</Warning>

### Currency and tax come from your account

You don't pick them product by product anymore:

- **Currency** is read-only. It's the currency your account uses in the product's country, and the screen says so: *"Set by the account for Colombia."* In **Brazil** it's always BRL.
- **Tax** is a single picker with your vendor's tax list. A product with no tax saved opens with the vendor's **default** already selected: *"The vendor's default tax was preloaded. It is stored when you save the price."* If what the product had saved isn't in the list, the screen asks you to choose one.

If the picker is empty, the account has no taxes defined for that vendor: they're set up in **Accounts › Countries & Vendors**.

<Info>
**Preloading a tax keeps the customer's price.** Since you type the gross, giving 19% tax to a product that had none leaves the price the customer pays exactly where it was — what drops is the net. If what you wanted was to add the tax on top, raise the gross.
</Info>

<Note>
**Brazil** keeps the tab short on purpose: the currency, the gross price, the suggested price and the **fiscal category (NCM)**. If you follow an instruction and can't find a field, that's probably why.
</Note>

### Suggested price: a struck price, never charged

**Suggested price** *(fantasy price)* is a reference the channel can show crossed out next to the real price — *"was 66.90, now 59.90"*. It is **never** what gets charged.

It is not a sale. A sale is a lower price that **is** charged, and it isn't set here: it's set in the **Sale price** column of the [price list](/en/manuals/backoffice/price-lists). Saving the product never erases a sale that lives on the list.

<Tip>
**Same screen for the customer, different data for you.** *12 Tiras* shows 59.90 with 66.90 struck through and **-10%** whether the 66.90 is a suggested price or the 59.90 is a sale. The difference is what reporting sees: a suggested price leaves a 59.90 product with no discount; a sale leaves a 66.90 product with a 7.00 discount — and only a sale reaches the combos the product is part of. If the -10% is a real promotion, make it a sale. See [Price lists](/en/manuals/backoffice/price-lists).
</Tip>

### Prices by fulfillment type

The **Prices by fulfillment type** card — *only on products, not combos* — gives one fulfillment type its own price: delivery pricier than dine-in, for example. *"By default every fulfillment type uses the base price. Add one to give it its own price."* With **Add fulfillment type** you pick which one; the rest show **Inherits base price**. Each fulfillment type you add gets its own gross, net and suggested price, the same three columns as the base price.

<Info>
**This is the catalog price, not the only price that exists.** What each store charges on each channel comes from the price list assigned to that destination. What you set here lands in the base list and is the starting point the others hang off. See [Price lists](/en/manuals/backoffice/price-lists).
</Info>

## Modifiers: what gets adjusted per product

On the **Modifier Groups** tab you add the groups you already defined in [Modifier Groups](/en/manuals/backoffice/modifier-groups).

<Warning>
**Adding a group copies it into the product.** From that moment, editing the group in [Modifier Groups](/en/manuals/backoffice/modifier-groups) no longer changes what this product asks. When the group has its own configuration per fulfillment type, the screen warns you before copying: *"It is copied to the product as is: from here on, editing the group no longer changes what this product asks."*
</Warning>

And here — for this product only — you can adjust the copy:

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/products/05-modificadores.png" alt="Modifier Groups tab with the per-product overrides" />
</Frame>

- The **title** the group is shown with.
- The **minimum** and the **maximum**. Whether the question is required isn't a separate switch: a minimum of 1 or more makes it required.
- The **price of each option** inside this product.

Whatever you change is marked **Custom for this product**.

### Different questions per fulfillment type

The selector is the record's **Fulfillment type** bar (only when **creating** the product does this tab carry a selector of its own). With the bar on a specific type, the tab shows *"what this product asks in"* that type, and anything you don't set there is inherited from **All fulfillment types**. On one type you can change the minimum and maximum, offer fewer options, or not ask the question at all: *"This question is not sent for this fulfillment type."* When you add a group with a type selected, the screen asks whether it applies **In every fulfillment type** or only in that one.

### Extras on a product

Questions with a minimum of 0 are **extras**: nothing is included and each one adds its full price. An extra can cost less inside this product than on its own. On *12 Tiras*, a Coca Cola 400 ml costs **9.90** as an extra, against 14.90 on its own — so *12 Tiras* + one Coca is **69.80**. If the standalone Coca goes on sale, the extra keeps its 9.90: a price set inside the product wins.

## Combos: what they hold and what they're worth

A combo is built on the **Composition** tab, grouping the products that make it up. It's the **Modifier Groups** tab under another name: each group is a decision in the combo — *pick your drink*, *pick your side* — and each group's minimum says how many units are included.

A combo **has no pricing tab**, and that's on purpose: what the customer pays is the sum of the options they pick, each at its price inside the combo on the destination's price list.

### The reference price: the "from" price

The **Reference price** card shows what the **cheapest configuration** costs: the cheapest option of each required group, times its minimum. On *Combo para 2*: the cheapest bucket (20.10) + 2 sandwiches at 8.60 + 2 drinks at 14.90 + a sauce at 5.80 = **72.90**. The breakdown is right there, group by group.

<Warning>
**The reference is not a fixed price you charge.** It's the starting point. A customer who picks pricier options pays more: on the channel, each option shows **what it adds** over the cheapest one of its group.

What actually gets charged comes from the destination's price list. See [Price lists](/en/manuals/backoffice/price-lists).
</Warning>

When an option is on sale, the card also shows the reference with sales underneath — *"On sale: 67.90"*. That's the price the channel shows as the combo's "from" price, with the regular reference struck through.

### Sale prices inside the combo

Each option with **Custom price** has a **Sale price in this combo** field. A sale that isn't lower than the option's price is flagged and not saved: *"Must be lower than the price. It will not be saved."*

An option **without** a custom price can't have a sale of its own here: it shows the standalone product's sale, read-only — *"From the product's own sale price in the base price list"* — because inside the combo it follows that product. Sales per fulfillment type are set on the [price list](/en/manuals/backoffice/price-lists).

### The combo's suggested price

Under the reference sits **Suggested price (fantasy price)**: *"Reference price shown to the customer, for example crossed out. It is not what is charged."* With the fulfillment type selector above the reference you give one fulfillment type its own suggested price; empty means *"uses the general one"*. Everything on this tab is saved together with **Save composition**.

If you leave the suggested price empty and an option's sale lowers the reference, the channel still gets a struck price: the reference **without** sales. A suggested price you type always wins over it.

<Warning>
**A combo with no questions in a fulfillment type goes out at zero there.** If you exclude every question for one fulfillment type, the screen warns you: *"This combo has no questions in … : it is published there with a reference price of 0."*
</Warning>

## Suggestions: upselling and cross-selling

Two different things, and the difference is what happens to the order:

| | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Upselling** | **Replaces** the order line. *"Want it large?"* |
| **Cross-selling** | **Adds** a new line. *"Add fries?"* |

Each fulfillment type can have its own lists. With the bar on a type, they start **inherited** from **All fulfillment types**; if you edit them, that type gets an own list that replaces the base one only there. See [Suggestions and schedule per type](#suggestions-and-schedule-per-type).

<Warning>
**A suggestion that isn't on the menu doesn't travel.** On sync, if the suggested product isn't part of that menu, the suggestion drops out of the dispatch and gets noted in the warnings. See [Menu assignment](/en/manuals/backoffice/menu-assignments).
</Warning>

## Images

**WebP, JPEG or PNG, up to 5 MB.** One image is the primary one, and that's the one used when there's no specific one.

The interesting part is that images can be **tagged per fulfillment type**: one photo for delivery, another for dine-in. The screen explains it: *"The primary image applies to every fulfillment type. Tag an image to customize a specific one."* The primary one is the fallback for every fulfillment type without its own.

  <img src="/images/manuals/backoffice/products/14-imagenes-por-tipo.png" alt="Images tab with the lens on Delivery: primary image, image tagged Shown in Delivery and the preview by fulfillment type" />

## Recipes: how the real cases get solved

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Adding a new dish and putting it on sale">
    1. **New product → New Product**. Name, code and save.
    2. **Categories** tab: assign its category.
    3. **Pricing** tab: the base price.
    4. **Images** tab: the photo, marked as primary.
    5. Go to [Menu assignment](/en/manuals/backoffice/menu-assignments) and sync the rows of the menu holding it.

    If the menu is the Golden and it was already assigned, the product goes in on its own: all that's missing is the sync.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Creating an extra that shouldn't appear on the menu">
    1. **New product → New Modifier**. Name it *Extra cheese*, with its price.
    2. In **General**, turn off **Show in menu**. The product stays active.
    3. Go to [Modifier Groups](/en/manuals/backoffice/modifier-groups) and add it as an option of the *Extras* group.

    It doesn't appear as a dish, but it does when the customer customizes.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Building a combo">
    1. **New product → New Combo**.
    2. **Composition** tab: group the products that make it up and define how many are picked from each group.
    3. Set what each option costs inside the combo on the [price list](/en/manuals/backoffice/price-lists) (**By combo** view), or with **Custom price** right here.
    4. Check the **Reference price**: it's the "from" price the customer will see.
    5. If you want a struck price next to it, fill **Suggested price** and **Save composition**.

    If you converted an existing product into a combo, careful: switching to Combo deletes the prices it had.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Putting a combo on sale">
    You don't discount the combo: you discount what's inside it.

    1. Pick the option that drives the price — usually the cheapest of a required group, like *4 Tiras + Batata Média* in *Combo para 2*.
    2. On **Composition**, turn on its **Custom price** and fill **Sale price in this combo** (20.10 → 15.10). Or do it on the price list, **By combo** view.
    3. **Save composition** and sync the menu in [Menu assignment](/en/manuals/backoffice/menu-assignments).

    The reference shows *"On sale: 67.90"* and the channel shows 67.90 with 72.90 struck through. Only customers who pick that option get the discount.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Showing a struck price on a product">
    Decide first whether it's a real discount:

    - **It's a promotion**: set the **Sale price** on the [price list](/en/manuals/backoffice/price-lists). It's charged, reporting sees it as a discount, and it also reaches the combos where the product has no price of its own.
    - **It's only a reference** (*"was 66.90"*): type the **Suggested price** on the **Pricing** tab. Nothing changes in what's charged.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Charging more for delivery than for dine-in">
    There are two paths and they aren't interchangeable:

    - **One specific product**: on the **Pricing** tab, **Prices by fulfillment type → Add fulfillment type**, and give delivery its own price.
    - **The whole menu**: don't do it product by product. Create a [price list](/en/manuals/backoffice/price-lists) linked to the base one with a formula, and assign it to the delivery rows.

    The second path is the one that scales and maintains itself.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Giving a product a different name for one fulfillment type">
    Example: *Combo Crunch Salad* goes out as «Combo Crunch Salad DIN» in Dine-In.

    1. Open the record and, on the **Fulfillment type** bar, pick **Dine-In**.
    2. On **General**, in the **Names and identifiers · Dine-In** card, type «Combo Crunch Salad DIN» in the name. The chip turns **custom** and the base name stays underneath.
    3. Repeat it in every language the base has (**PT · ES · EN**).
    4. If the marketing description should differ too, change it there; whatever you leave untouched stays **inherited**.
    5. Save.
    6. Go to [Menu assignment](/en/manuals/backoffice/menu-assignments) and **sync the affected rows**.

    The last step isn't optional: changing the record doesn't flag the rows as out of date, and until you sync them the channel keeps the old name.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Showing a product in a different category for one type">
    Example: *Batata Grande* is in **ACOMPANHAMENTOS** in the base, but in Dine-In it should only appear in **REFEIÇÃO**.

    1. Open the record. On **Categories**, with **All fulfillment types**, the base list says **ACOMPANHAMENTOS**.
    2. Pick **Dine-In** on the bar.
    3. Take out ACOMPANHAMENTOS and add **REFEIÇÃO**. The list turns **custom**, with *Base: ACOMPANHAMENTOS* underneath.
    4. **Save categories**.
    5. Go back to **All fulfillment types**: the **By Fulfillment Type** summary now says *Dine-In → REFEIÇÃO*.
    6. Sync the affected rows in [Menu assignment](/en/manuals/backoffice/menu-assignments).

    Dine-In rows publish it in REFEIÇÃO only; Takeaway and Delivery keep ACOMPANHAMENTOS.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Hiding a product in one type only">
    1. On the bar, pick the fulfillment type where it shouldn't appear.
    2. On **General**, turn off **Visible in \{type\}**.
    3. Save and sync the rows of that type in [Menu assignment](/en/manuals/backoffice/menu-assignments).

    The product is hidden in that type in every menu, and stays as it was in the others. If a specific menu does need it there, that menu can show it again.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Taking a product off sale">
    It depends how long for:

    - **For a while, because it ran out**: [Out of stock](/en/manuals/backoffice/out-of-stock). It comes back on its own.
    - **This season**: turn off **Show in menu**. It stays available inside combos.
    - **For good**: set the product **inactive**, which takes it out of every menu. Deleting is permanent and cannot be undone.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Worked example: one catalog, three rows

The Golden Menu has a row for each fulfillment type. Suppose you do two things on the record. For *Combo Crunch Salad*, you give Dine-In the name «Combo Crunch Salad DIN» and Takeaway «Combo Crunch Salad TkOUT». For *Batata Grande*, you give Dine-In its own category list with only **REFEIÇÃO**; the base stays **ACOMPANHAMENTOS** and Takeaway and Delivery inherit it.

| Golden Menu row | Combo Crunch Salad · before | Combo Crunch Salad · after | Batata Grande · before | Batata Grande · after |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Dine-In** | Combo Crunch Salad | **Combo Crunch Salad DIN** | ACOMPANHAMENTOS | **REFEIÇÃO** only |
| **Takeaway** | Combo Crunch Salad | **Combo Crunch Salad TkOUT** | ACOMPANHAMENTOS | ACOMPANHAMENTOS |
| **Delivery** | Combo Crunch Salad | Combo Crunch Salad *(base name)* | ACOMPANHAMENTOS | ACOMPANHAMENTOS |

Delivery has no configuration of its own, so it goes out exactly as before. And none of this counts until the rows are synced.

## Mistakes that cost money

<Warning>
**Editing the record and not syncing.** In **Menu assignment** the row is **not** flagged as out of date when you change the record — just as it isn't when you change the base name or the price. Until you sync the affected rows, the channel keeps the old name, category or visibility.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**Leaving a type with no categories.** An empty list in a type means *"the product does not appear in this fulfillment type"*: the product disappears from every menu in that type. If that wasn't the goal, use **revert to inherited**.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**Loading the type's text in only one language.** The published name prefers Spanish when the base has it. If you load the Dine-In name only in PT, Dine-In keeps going out with the base name in Spanish. Load it in every language the base has.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**Changing the type without looking at the cost.** Switching to **Combo** deletes the product's prices and switching to **Modifier** removes its groups. And with **Packaging** there's no way back: it isn't in the selector.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**Leaving a modifier with "Show in menu" on.** *Extra cheese* appears on the menu as if it were a dish.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**Setting the price here and expecting it to reach the channel.** This is the catalog price. What each destination charges comes from its price list, and it reaches nowhere until the row is synced.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**Using the suggested price as if it were a sale.** The customer sees the same struck price, but nothing is discounted in reporting and the combos the product belongs to don't get it. A promotion goes in the **Sale price** column of the price list.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**Editing a modifier group and expecting the products that use it to change.** Adding a group copies it into the product: later changes to the group's title, limits or options don't reach that copy. See [Modifier Groups](/en/manuals/backoffice/modifier-groups) for how to bring a change into the products that already have it.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**Leaving a combo with no questions in a fulfillment type.** It's published there with a reference of 0.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**Writing internal notes in the marketing description.** That text gets published and the customer reads it.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**Suggesting a product that isn't on the menu.** The suggestion drops out of the dispatch silently: it's only noted in the sync warnings.
</Warning>

## Glossary

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Product** | An item sold directly. |
| **Combo** | A bundle made up of several products. |
| **Modifier** | A product that exists to be another one's option. |
| **Packaging** | The container associated with a product. |
| **Code / PLU** | The point-of-sale operational code. |
| **Show in menu** | Whether it appears on the menu. Off, it keeps working in combos and modifiers. |
| **Commercial state** | **Available**, **Out of stock** or **Not sellable**. The system calculates it; it isn't editable. |
| **Channel Exclusive** | The product leaves the Golden Menu and only exists where you add it by hand. |
| **Reference price** *(ref.)* | On a combo, what the cheapest configuration costs: the "from" price. Not a fixed price. |
| **Gross price** | What the customer pays, taxes included. The only price you type; the net is calculated. |
| **Suggested price** | A reference shown crossed out. Never charged. |
| **Sale price** | A lower price that **is** charged. Set on the price list, not here. |
| **Upselling / Cross-selling** | Replacing the order line, or adding a new one. |
| **Fulfillment type lens** | Choosing a type on the **Fulfillment type** bar: the record is then edited for that type only. |
| **Custom / Inherited / Shared** | Chips on each field: its own value for the type · same as **All fulfillment types** · it doesn't vary by type. |
| **Own list** | The categories or suggestions a type defines for itself. It replaces the base list only in that type; it isn't added to it. |

## Frequently asked questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="I created the product and it doesn't show in the app">
    Walk it in order: is it active with **Show in menu** on? is it in a visible category? is it in the menu assigned to that store and channel? was that row synced after you created it? The last step is the one most often forgotten — see [Menu assignment](/en/manuals/backoffice/menu-assignments).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I changed the name for delivery and the app still shows the old one">
    Three things to check, in this order. **Did you sync?** Changing the record doesn't flag the rows in [Menu assignment](/en/manuals/backoffice/menu-assignments) as out of date: you have to sync the Delivery rows by hand. **Did you save** with the lens on Delivery? (the Delivery dot on the bar should be filled). And **languages**: if the base has Spanish and the Delivery name is only in another language, the base name in Spanish still goes out.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What is the red dot next to a fulfillment type?">
    It isn't an alert. There's one dot per type: **filled** means that type has its own configuration, saved; **hollow** means it inherits everything from **All fulfillment types**. It's brand-colored, and a change you haven't saved doesn't light it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I hide a product in one type but show it in a specific menu?">
    Yes. **Visible in \{type\}** turned off hides it in that type in every menu, but a menu can still show it again in that type.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Why does the combo show a price with 'ref.'?">
    Because it isn't a fixed price: it's what the cheapest configuration costs — the cheapest option of each required group, up to its minimum. A customer who picks pricier options pays more. The prices come from the destination's price list.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I picked the wrong type, how do I change it?">
    With the selector in the record's header, if it's between **Modifier**, **Product** and **Combo**. Mind the cost: switching to Combo deletes the prices and switching to Modifier removes the modifier groups.

    If you got it wrong with **Packaging**, there's no selector: you have to create the product again.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Where do I put a product on sale?">
    Not on this screen: in the **Sale price** column of the [price list](/en/manuals/backoffice/price-lists). The **Pricing** tab only has the **Suggested price**, which is a struck reference and is never charged.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I changed the tax and the price didn't move">
    That's expected: you type the gross — what the customer pays — and the net is calculated from it. A higher tax lowers the net, not the customer's price. To pass the tax on, raise the gross.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The pricing and image tabs are greyed out">
    You still have to save. Until then the product doesn't exist: *"Save the product first to access this section"*.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What's the difference between inactive, hidden and out of stock?">
    **Inactive** takes it out of every menu and it cannot be sold. **Hidden** (**Show in menu** off) takes it off the menu but leaves it available inside combos and modifiers. **Out of stock** is temporary and may show greyed out. For something that ran out mid-service, the right move is [Out of stock](/en/manuals/backoffice/out-of-stock), which comes back on its own.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
